# VII. THE STAR VAULT — *the night everything came apart*

---

The Stars knew before Lumi did.

He was halfway through the evening count — a ritual so old and so dull that no
Keeper in history has admitted finding it comforting, and all of them have —
when the Meadow Star went out of rhythm.

Not bright. Not dim. **Off-beat.** Like a heart deciding, mid-stride, that it
had changed its mind about something.

Then Crystal flickered. Then Frost. Then Ember, which never flickered, which had
not flickered once in the four years Lumi had carried it.

And then the Shadow fastening — the one that had been empty his whole life, the
one he had never asked anybody about because some part of him already knew not
to — began to pulse.

Lumi put a paw to his scarf.

"Hey. Hey — what's wrong?"

The Stars did not answer. They had never once failed to answer.

Then every light in the Vault went out.

Not dimmed. Not guttered. **Stopped**, all together, like a held note released.

And the dark that replaced them was not the dark of a room with no lamp in it.
It had *weight*. It had grain. It leaned.

A sound came up through the floor. Not footsteps — something heavier and much
more patient, a vibration Lumi felt in the bones of his legs before he heard it
at all.

Two pale violet eyes opened between the pillars, at a height Lumi had to lift
his chin to find.

The thing they belonged to walked out of the dark, and the dark came with it.

Enormous. Black in a way that was not a colour — his fur did not reflect the
Vault's residual glow so much as *decline* it, so that he read as a hole cut in
the room. Thin violet fractures crawled across him like lightning caught under
stone. Smoke lifted off his outline and did not disperse.

And at the centre of his chest, a cracked violet gem, pulsing in time with the
empty tenth fastening on Lumi's scarf.

Lumi had never seen him. Had never been told his name.

Every hair on his spine stood up anyway.

The huge cat stopped six paces out and looked, for a long moment, at nothing but
the scarf.

When he spoke, the voice filled the Vault without being raised — low, unhurried,
and so tired that the menace in it seemed almost like an afterthought.

> **NOCTRA:** Ten lights.

Lumi backed up one step. His paw found a pillar.

> **NOCTRA:** Still pretending they belong to you.

> **LUMI:** They don't belong to anyone.

Something happened in the enormous cat's face. Not surprise — *recognition*,
the specific twitch of a man hearing his own words come back at him after a very
long time. It was gone almost at once.

> **NOCTRA:** No. They don't.
> *(a beat)*
> Then it costs you nothing to give them to me.

> **LUMI:** That's not how that works.

> **NOCTRA:** Isn't it. Explain it to me.

> **LUMI:** They don't belong to me. So I can't give them away. That's the
> *whole point*. That's — that's the entire job, that's the first thing you
> learn —

He heard himself and stopped. He had no idea where he had learned it. He had
never been able to name the person who taught him. The thought came and went in
under a second and he would not remember having had it for a very long time.

Noctra had gone very still.

> **NOCTRA:** *(quietly)* Who taught you that?

> **LUMI:** ...I don't know.

> **NOCTRA:** No.
> *(and something in the huge cat's shoulders changed)*
> You wouldn't.

Then he moved.

---

The Vault came apart.

Shadow tore across the floor in a sheet and Lumi went sideways off the pillar,
and the gold woke in his forelegs and his brow, and the light he threw was not a
weapon, it was a **wall** — and the wall held, and the shadow broke around it,
and the mosaics four hundred years older than either of them shattered into the
dark in pieces the size of teeth.

Noctra struck again. And again. He was stronger — not because he had more power,
though he did, but because he had been rehearsing this for four hundred years and
he had never once, in all that time, had to consider what it would actually feel
like.

Lumi gave ground and gave ground and the Stars screamed through his scarf in
pulses of colour — green, silver, blue, red, white, turquoise, pale
aurora — and he kept doing the one thing that made no sense.

He did not hit back.

Barriers. Deflections. Light shaped to push away rather than burn. Every single
motion defensive, and it was costing him the fight, and it was obvious, and it
was clearly deliberate.

Noctra brought his weight down and the barrier went to one knee under it and he
put his face close to the smaller cat's and said, with what sounded almost like
anger at being cheated:

> **NOCTRA:** Why won't you *fight?*

The gold barrier cracked. Lumi's legs were shaking. He looked straight up into
the violet and he said it like it was obvious, like it was the least remarkable
statement in the world:

> **LUMI:** Because you're angry.

> **NOCTRA:** *(flat)* Everything is angry. The wind is angry. It still kills
> people.

> **LUMI:** Yeah. But you *want* something.
> *(the barrier splintering, his voice going thin)*
> Things that just want to kill you don't stop to tell you they're
> disappointed.

And then, because he could not hold the barrier and think at the same time, and
because it was the only thing he had left:

> **LUMI:** You're angry. That doesn't mean you're supposed to die.

The Vault went quiet.

It was not a pause. It was a **stop** — Noctra's entire weight lifted off the
barrier, and he did not seem to have decided to do it.

A single hairline fracture opened across the violet gem in his chest.

It was gold.

And in the depth of it, for less than a second, something moved that had not
moved in four hundred years. Not violet. Not shadow.

**Starlight.**

Noctra looked down at his own chest.

Then he looked at Lumi — really looked, for the first time — at a small white
cat on one knee under a broken barrier, with gold filigree on his forelegs that
nobody alive had taught him to earn, wearing a scarf of Eclipse-silk woven by
hands that were currently attached to the creature about to kill him.

> **NOCTRA:** *(barely audible)* ...Don't.

> **LUMI:** Don't what?

> **NOCTRA:** Don't *say* things like that.

> **LUMI:** Why?

And Noctra roared.

---

It was not rage. Anyone watching closely would have known. It was the sound a
creature makes when the thing it has spent four centuries building is coming
apart in its hands and the only available response is **noise**.

Shadow erupted through the Vault, floor to ceiling, and a claw of it came down
across Lumi's chest — and the scarf.

The constellation broke.

Ten fastenings tore open at once.

---

And the Ten Stars, who had been waiting for this for longer than anyone knew,
understood what was about to be asked of them.

Not *captured*. That was never the fear. They had been captured before and
survived it.

**Gathered.**

So they did the thing no living creature had ever seen all ten of them do
together.

**HOMING.**

The Meadow Star went first, a streak of green-gold that took a hole through the
Vault roof like it was cloth.

Moon vanished into silver. Crystal went blue and went **through** the eastern
wall. Ember burned red and the stone it passed through was still glowing an hour
later. Sky flashed white. Neon cracked turquoise across the ceiling. Frost drew a
line of pale aurora that hung in the air after it had gone.

Three more lights left so fast that Lumi, who had carried them, could not have
said what colour they were.

Ten beams tore out into the night over oceans, over forests, over mountains,
over deserts, over ruins that had been empty since before the lie.

Meadow to Whispering Meadow.
Moon to Moonlit Woods.
Crystal across the sea to Crystal Coast.
Ember down into Ember Canyon.
Sky climbing to Skygarden Peaks.
Neon under the canopy of the Rainforest.
Frost north, to the valley that would not thaw again.
Light home to the Celestial Ruins, to the blank walls it had made blank.
Shadow — straight down, to Eclipse Sanctuary, to the dark it had been taken
from.

And Celestial crossed the edge of every map that has ever been drawn, into the
Astral Wilds.

Noctra stood in the wreckage and watched them go.

For the first time, the control broke and something raw came through.

> **NOCTRA:** No. **No** —

Four hundred years. Every calculation. Every rehearsal.

He had come for ten Stars.

He now had **none**.

---

Lumi tried to get up.

The scarf hung off his neck. Dark. Empty. Ten small silver rings and nothing in
any of them.

Noctra crossed the floor toward him.

Lumi lifted a paw and reached for a barrier and there was nothing there.

Not weakened. **Absent.** The place where it lived was gone.

And then something else started to go.

A name.

He had a name. He was reaching for it and it was —

A face. There had been a face, someone's face, and he knew that it mattered
enormously and he could not have said whether it was male or female or whether he
had seen it yesterday or when he was small.

A place. He had been here before. He knew he had been here before the way you
know a word is in your mouth. When? *When?*

The Ten Stars had never held Lumi's memories.

They had held the **addresses**.

He looked around the ruined Vault, at a chamber he had walked into an hour ago,
and found that he had no idea where he was.

He looked at the enormous black cat standing over him — and he knew, with total
certainty and no content whatsoever, that this creature was the most important
thing in his life.

He could not remember why.

He tried to remember who had given him his scarf.

Nothing. Not a blank — *nothing*. Not even the shape of where an answer had been.

> **LUMI:** ...Where...

His front legs folded.

> **LUMI:** ...am I?

---

And Noctra stopped walking.

The violet eyes came down to the small white shape on the broken floor, and
everything that had been in his face — four hundred years of it — simply went
out.

He had prepared for a great many outcomes.

Not this one.

> **NOCTRA:** *(very quietly)* What did you do.

There was no one to answer. Lumi could not hear him.

> **NOCTRA:** You were supposed to *hold onto* them. That is the entire —
> *(he stops)*
> That is what I —

He did not finish it.

He knelt. An enormous creature made of swallowed light, folding down onto broken
mosaic beside a cat a fraction of his size, and he reached out, and the smoke
came off his paw in a long slow curl, and he did not touch him.

Voices echoed from the upper galleries. Lantern-light, swinging, distant.
Somebody shouting for the Keeper.

Noctra's head came up toward the dark.

He looked back down.

Lumi's eyes were half-shut and going. He was still, very faintly, humming
something. Four notes, low and slow, unhurried. He had no idea he was doing it.
He had no idea what it was.

Noctra knew what it was.

He had written it.

For a long moment the Keeper of the Interval knelt in the wreckage of a plan four
centuries old and listened to a cat who had forgotten his own name hum a lullaby
back at him.

Then he stood up.

> **NOCTRA:** Find them.

Lumi's eyes moved. Nothing behind them.

Noctra stepped backward, and the dark came forward to meet him, and everything
except the violet went out.

> **NOCTRA:** And when you remember —

A long pause. Long enough to be a different sentence than the one he started.

> **NOCTRA:** — find me.

Then the eyes closed and the Vault was only a ruin.

Lumi did not hear the last part.

He was already unconscious.

---

*It was not a threat.*

*Nobody in Veylora will believe that for a very long time, including Lumi, and
including — on most days — Noctra.*
