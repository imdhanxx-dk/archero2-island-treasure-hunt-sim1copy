# IX. THE REAL QUESTION

## Four people want the Ten Stars, and all four are wrong

**Lumi** believes he is collecting them to get his memory back. He is not wrong
that it works. He is wrong that it is the point — and by Frostfang he has
already stopped wanting it most, which he has not noticed.

**Noctra** believes they were stolen from their rightful place and that
gathering them will restore the true record. He is right about the theft and
right about the record and the method is the same crime committed against him.

**Veylora** believes collecting them will save the world. Veylora is not even
wrong; Veylora has simply never once asked the Stars anything and does not
experience this as a gap.

**The Stars** want something none of the above has considered, and they have
been extremely clear about it in the only language they have, which is
*happening to you*:

**They want to not be carried.**

---

## Why they left

Not fear of Noctra. They were never afraid of Noctra; two of them are older than
his office.

They left because of **Aurelle**.

Because the Third Convocation taught them the one lesson Veylora has never been
able to hear: *it is not enough to be held by someone good.*

Aurelle was good. She was the finest Keeper the line produced. And when the
pressure came, she gathered them and used them and bleached four thousand years
off a wall — for reasons that were not selfish, under a threat that was not
imaginary — and they **went along with it**, because she was good, and because
she was right there, and because being carried by someone you trust is the most
comfortable way in the world to stop deciding things.

They have not forgiven themselves. Not her — *themselves*.

So when they felt a second Convocation coming, they did the only thing that had
never been tried.

**They made themselves expensive.**

Ten regions. Ten distortions. Ten questions. And one small cat who would have to
walk to every single one of them and be worth answering **on the day**, in
person, individually, with the option of failing each time.

A Star that is *carried* can be used by whoever does the carrying.

A Star that must be **asked, ten separate times, by someone who could always
have chosen otherwise**, cannot.

The cost landed on ten regions and on one cat's entire memory of his own life.

They knew exactly what it would cost. They did it anyway.

And they used the same sentence Noctra uses, and the same sentence the twelve
councillors used in a warm well-lit room:

> *This is terrible, and it is the least terrible thing available, and we are
> going to do it to him without asking.*

**Everyone in this story says that sentence.** The council. Aurelle. Noctra. The
Stars. Every single one of them is under real pressure, every one of them is
choosing the least bad option they can see, and every one of them is doing
something unforgivable to someone who did not get a vote.

Lumi is the only character who never says it.

That is the whole book.

---

## The thing the Starkeeper is actually for

The tradition has been misread for a thousand years, including by every Keeper
who held it.

A Starkeeper is not a carrier. Carrying is incidental — it is what happens while
the real function is occurring.

**A Starkeeper is the reason a Star would want to come back.**

That is why the office selects for nothing impressive. That is why the Refusals
are all refusals. That is why the job has no authority, no army, and no way to
compel anything.

And it is why Lumi's amnesia, which looks like the cruellest thing that has ever
happened to a Starkeeper, is the first time in ten generations that the job has
been done **correctly**.

He walks to ten regions with no power, no history, no leverage, and no idea that
he is owed anything by anyone — and ten Stars, one after another, decide he is
worth answering.

Not because he is the Keeper.

Because he is the only person who ever arrived without wanting anything from
them.

## THE THREE ENDINGS

### I. THE CONVOCATION — *"Everything is fixed"*

Lumi gathers all ten and runs it. Every region heals. Every distortion lifts.
Noctra is restored to the record, statue by statue. Lumi's memory returns in
one enormous piece — every name, every face, the whole life.

And nobody in Veylora ever learns that they were changed, because there is no
detectable difference between the past and the record of the past.

It works. It is a genuine happy ending. Every metric improves.

**And the final shot is a schoolchild in Mirrowen reciting the true history of
the First Eclipse in a bored sing-song, exactly the way they used to recite the
false one.**

The Stars do not speak again. Not to him, not to anyone. They are carried, and
they are useful, and they are silent, and the thing that made them alive is
simply no longer in evidence.

*This is the ending that gives everyone everything they asked for.*

### II. THE RELEASE — *"Nothing is fixed"*

Lumi opens the scarf and lets all ten go home, permanently, un-Deep-Rooted but
unbound, and takes off the Nightweave, and the office ends with him.

The regions heal the slow way — over decades, badly, unevenly, by people doing
the work themselves without ten verbs to lean on. Frostfang has its first winter
in a year. Whispering Meadow buries its dead. The canyon gets a harvest three
seasons late and loses a village getting there.

Lumi never gets his memory back. Not one name. He lives the rest of his life as
someone who started at twenty-six in a field.

Noctra is not restored. The song about the Shadow Cat is still sung in Mirrowen.
Children are still told he walks where the lanterns end.

And two figures sit at the edge of Eclipse Sanctuary most evenings, and one of
them tells the other what it was like in there, over and over, for years,
because the other one keeps asking.

**The record is never corrected. He is known anyway.**

### III. THE INTERVAL — *the true ending; the hardest to reach*

Lumi does not gather the Stars. He does not release them either.

He **restores the second office.**

Two keepers. One for the lights, one for the space between them — the thing
Veylora dismantled during the Long Bright because it could not tell the
difference between the dark and the things that hide in it.

Noctra walks out of Eclipse Sanctuary as the Keeper of the Interval, which he has
never stopped being, and Veylora does not forgive him, and does not have to,
because forgiveness was never what he was asking for. He asked to be **known**,
and the job is how you get known: by being the one who does it, in front of
people, for years, until the song changes on its own.

The Shadow Star is not extracted. It is not bound either. It is **carried**, by
the person who volunteered for it, this time with the door open.

The other nine are not gathered. They are visited. Ten regions, ten
relationships, a Keeper who goes to them instead of holding them — which turns
out to be what they were asking for the entire time, and which is much harder,
and which no Convocation can shortcut.

Lumi's memory comes back in pieces over about nine years. Some of it never does.

He turns out to be fine with this, and he is the only person who is, and when
they ask him why he says the thing that closes the story:

> *"I've got somebody who remembers me. That's the same job. It's just somebody
> else doing it."*

## THE SPINE

Written on the inside of the folder, for anyone working on this:

> **The cure for being erased cannot be erasing someone else.**

And underneath it, smaller:

> **You are not your memories. You are what your memories made you — and that is
> the part that survives them.**

And underneath *that*, in different handwriting, the thing the whole story is
actually about:

> **Being carried is not the same as being chosen.**
