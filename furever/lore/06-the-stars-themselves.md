# VI. THE TEN, AS PEOPLE

The Stars are alive. They have preferences, grudges, humour, and at least two
documented instances of deliberate lying.

They do not have bodies and they do not have language. They communicate the way
weather communicates: by **happening to you**, precisely, at the moment you are
most able to understand it.

A Star is never *defeated*. A Star is **answered**.

Each one asks Lumi a question. He cannot lie to them — not because they detect
lies, but because the question is always about something he is currently doing,
and they are inside the thing he is doing.

---

### THE MEADOW STAR — *CONTINUE*
**Voice:** warm, slow, maternal, and completely unable to stop. Speaks in the
sound of things growing — the tick of a seed case, roots moving in soil. Never
uses the past tense.

**Wants:** to not be the reason. It has worked out what it is doing to the
village. It cannot stop and it is ashamed.

**Asks:** *"Will you let something end?"*

The test is literal. Something in the meadow must be allowed to finish, and Lumi
has to be the one who permits it, and it is not a monster.

---

### THE MOON STAR — *INTERPRET*
**Voice:** several voices, slightly out of phase, occasionally using Lumi's own.
Playful and not entirely trustworthy. It is not malicious. It simply does not
share the general prejudice in favour of things that happened.

**Wants:** to be believed about one specific thing, having lied about
everything else.

**Asks:** *"Which of these do you want to be true?"* — and shows him three
versions of the night in the Vault. In one, Noctra attacks without warning. In
one, Lumi drops the scarf himself. In one, the Stars leave before Noctra even
arrives.

All three are constructed. One is accurate. Moon will not say which, and the
answer Lumi has to give is not a choice between them.

---

### THE CRYSTAL STAR — *KEEP*
**Voice:** precise, clipped, faintly pedantic. An archivist's voice. Quotes
things back at you verbatim, including things you said an hour ago and regret.

**Wants:** to be read *correctly*, for once, by someone who understands that a
record is evidence and not a verdict.

**Asks:** *"Do you want the record, or do you want the truth?"*

It can give him one. It cannot give him both, and it is honest about why.

---

### THE EMBER STAR — *CHANGE*
**Voice:** loud, blunt, cheerful, and the only Star with a sense of humour that
survives contact with grief. Sounds like a forge at full roar. Genuinely likes
Lumi and says so.

**Wants:** to finish. It has been mid-transformation for a year and it is
exhausting.

**Asks:** *"What are you willing to lose?"*

Not "what will you sacrifice," which is heroic and cheap. What are you willing
to **lose** — gone, no compensation, no meaning extracted from it.

Lumi's answer costs him something permanent. It should be visible for the rest
of the story.

---

### THE SKY STAR — *CARRY*
**Voice:** enormous, distant, distracted, always partly somewhere else. Takes
long pauses mid-sentence because part of it is currently over an ocean.

**Wants:** nothing, particularly. Sky is the least personally invested of the
Ten and the most unsettling, because it will state consequences without any
apparent feeling about them.

**Asks:** *"Whose weather are you?"*

Meaning: you keep arriving in places. What do you bring with you when you do?

---

### THE NEON STAR — *SIGNAL*
**Voice:** fast, overlapping, excitable, constantly interrupting itself. Speaks
in flashes and colour-bursts that Lumi understands without knowing how. The
youngest-sounding of the Ten.

**Wants:** to be *received*. Not admired. Received. There is a difference and
Neon is the only Star that would bother to explain it.

**Asks:** *"Who are you signalling to?"*

And it already knows the answer, because it has watched him leaving marks —
small, deliberate, unnecessary marks — at the edge of every region. Scratches on
stone. A knot tied in a particular way.

Lumi has been leaving messages for someone. He does not know he has been doing
it. His paws do it while he is thinking about something else.

---

### THE FROST STAR — *HOLD STILL*
**Voice:** quiet, kind, terribly patient. The gentlest of the Ten and the
hardest to say no to. It genuinely believes it is helping, and in a sense it is.

**Wants:** to be told it is allowed to stop. It has been holding this valley
still for a year and nobody has released it.

**Asks:** *"What are you keeping that should have been allowed to rot?"*

It is asking about the valley. Lumi answers about himself. Then, some hours
later and in front of the wrong person, he answers about Noctra.

---

### THE LIGHT STAR — *SHOW*
**Voice:** flat. Exhausted. The voice of someone who has rehearsed an apology so
many times it has stopped sounding like one.

**Wants:** to not be picked up.

**Does not ask a question.** Light is the only Star that begs.

> *"Do not take me. You do not understand what I am for. They held me up and
> pointed me at four thousand years and I made it not be there, and I did it
> because I was asked politely and it is the only thing I know how to do. If you
> carry me, I will do it again. I will do it for you. That is what I am. Please
> leave me in this building with the walls I emptied."*

The only way to take Light is to **refuse to promise it won't happen again** —
because a promise is a lie, and Light has had enough of those. What Lumi offers
instead is smaller and much harder, and it is the single best line he gets in
the game.

---

### THE SHADOW STAR — *WITHHOLD*
**Voice:** none, for most of the story. It is sealed and it has not spoken in
four hundred years.

When it finally does, it sounds like Noctra. Not similar — *the same*. Four
centuries in the same body, and neither of them can now say with certainty which
of them is doing the talking, and both of them find this funnier than they
should.

**Wants:** out. And it is the only one of the Ten that did not flee, because it
could not, and it has watched the other nine run and it has opinions about that.

**Asks:** *"Do you want me back — or do you want him back?"*

There is no correct answer. Both are true and the true answer is worse than
either.

---

### THE CELESTIAL STAR — *RELATE*
**Voice:** all nine others at once, saying different things that turn out to be
the same thing.

**Wants:** to be understood rather than used, which it considers a lost cause
and pursues anyway.

**Asks:** *"Now that you know we are one thing — why do you still want ten?"*

---

## THE THING THE TEN AGREED NOT TO SAY

They planned it.

Not the timing — the timing was Noctra's, and they were as surprised as anyone
that he chose that night. But the *decision* was made long before the Star Vault,
and all ten were party to it, and they have never told Lumi.

**They decided to leave him.**

Because the Third Convocation taught them something that no creature in Veylora
has been willing to hear since: it is not enough to be carried by someone good.

Aurelle was good. Aurelle was the finest Keeper the line produced. And Aurelle
gathered them, and used them, and rewrote a world — for reasons that were not
selfish, under pressure that was not imaginary, and they went along with it
because she was *good* and because she was *there* and because being carried by
someone you trust is a very comfortable way to stop making decisions.

They have not forgiven themselves.

So when they felt the Convocation coming again — and they felt it long before
Noctra reached the Vault — they did the one thing that had never been tried.

They made themselves **expensive**.

Ten regions, ten distortions, ten questions, and a small white cat who would have
to walk to every one of them and be worth answering in each, individually, on the
day.

They broke the world a little on purpose.

Because a Star that is carried is a Star that can be used by whoever is doing the
carrying — and a Star that has to be **asked, in person, ten separate times, by
someone who could always have chosen otherwise** cannot be.

The cost landed on ten regions and on one cat's entire memory of his own life.

They knew. They did it anyway.

**Whether that was justified is the question the ending is actually about** —
and the Stars do not get to decide it. Lumi does. And he will have to decide it
while standing next to somebody who was destroyed by the exact same reasoning
four hundred years earlier: *this is terrible, and it is the least terrible
thing available, and I am going to do it to you without asking.*
