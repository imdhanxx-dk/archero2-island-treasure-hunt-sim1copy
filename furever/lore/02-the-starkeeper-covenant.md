# II. THE STARKEEPER COVENANT

## The job nobody wants once they understand it

A Starkeeper is not a wielder.

A Starkeeper is not a champion, a chosen one, a vessel, or a weapon. The office
has no army, no territory, no salary, and — this surprises people — no
authority over the Stars whatsoever.

A Starkeeper is a **companion with a very specific responsibility**: to be
worth travelling with.

That is the whole of it. The Stars go where they wish. A Keeper's entire
craft is to be the kind of creature that ten living forces would *choose* to
stay near, and to notice, faster than anyone else, when one of them has stopped
choosing it.

The primer given to Keeper-children opens with four sentences. They are called
the Refusals, and they are phrased as refusals rather than duties because every
one of them is a thing you will badly want to do.

---

## THE FOUR REFUSALS

### The First Refusal — **You may not own.**

*Carrying is not possessing.*

The Stars rest in the scarf the way a bird rests on a branch: entirely by its
own decision, and the branch does not get a say. A Keeper who begins to think of
the Stars as *theirs* has already lost them; the loss simply has not finished
arriving yet.

Apprentices are made to give away something they love in their first year. Not
symbolically. Actually. They do not get it back.

### The Second Refusal — **You may not command.**

*You may ask. You may never order.*

There is no known way to force a Star. There are, however, many known ways to
*pressure* one — to arrange circumstances so narrow that the Star's choice is
technically free and practically inevitable. Every one of these is forbidden,
and every one of them has been used.

The distinction between asking and cornering is the hardest thing a Keeper ever
learns, because from the inside they feel identical. Both feel like need.

### The Third Refusal — **You may not keep what asks to leave.**

*When a Star wants out, the scarf opens. Immediately. Without argument.*

Not after you have made your case. Not after you have explained that the timing
is terrible, that the region needs it, that people will die. Immediately.

Keepers have died holding a scarf open in a situation where closing it would
have saved them. They are the ones the tradition is named after.

### The Fourth Refusal — *(struck from the oath 400 years ago)*

**You may not call them back.**

A Keeper may carry a Star. Protect a Star. Return a Star to its homeland when it
has become unstable. A Keeper may wait, indefinitely, in the hope that a Star
comes back on its own.

A Keeper may not **summon**.

Because a summoned Star is a commanded Star wearing better manners. The Fourth
Refusal is the one that makes the other three mean anything — without it,
"you may not own" is merely a delay, and "you may not command" is merely a
politeness.

It was removed from the oath in the year of the First Eclipse.

It had to be. The Redaction required calling them back.

Every Keeper trained after that year learned three Refusals and was told,
truthfully, that this was the complete oath. Lumi learned four. He does not
remember learning them, and he cannot tell you where the fourth came from.

It came from a voice underground.

---

## THE NIGHTWEAVE

The scarf.

Every Starkeeper has one. No two are alike. And there is exactly one thing
about them that no book printed in the last four hundred years will tell you:

**The scarf is made of Shadow.**

The thread is **Eclipse-silk** — spun in absolute darkness, in the deep
galleries beneath Eclipse Sanctuary, by a process that requires the total
absence of light for eleven days. It is not dyed dark. It is not black cloth.
It is *woven absence*, and when you hold it the light in the room does not fall
on it so much as decline to.

The reason is not aesthetic. It is optical, and it is the entire thesis of the
Tenfold Sky compressed into a garment:

> **You cannot see a star without something for it to shine against.**

Ten of the most brilliant forces in existence, set on any other fabric, would
simply drown each other. Set against woven night, each one is distinct,
locatable, *legible*. The scarf does not contain the Stars.

The scarf **makes them visible**.

And it was always made by the same hands. Not the Starkeeper's. The
**Nightkeeper's**. The two offices were a pair, and the physical proof of that
partnership hung around the neck of every Starkeeper in history, in plain view,
for centuries, and nobody looked at it twice after the name was scrubbed.

Lumi has been wearing Noctra's last piece of work since he was a kitten.

---

## THE TEN FASTENINGS

Ten small circular clasps, stitched in silver.

They are not arranged in a standard pattern. There is no standard pattern.

Each Keeper's fastenings shift, slowly, over a lifetime, settling into an
arrangement unique to that Keeper — a constellation that exists nowhere in the
sky and belongs to exactly one person. It cannot be designed. It cannot be
copied. It forms the way a signature forms: out of ten thousand unconsidered
repetitions.

By the time a Keeper is grown, the pattern *is* them.

---

## THE COORDINATE SYSTEM — how the scarf holds a life

This is the mechanism at the centre of the story, so it is worth being precise
about what it does and — more importantly — what it does not do.

**The scarf does not store memories.**

It is not a diary, a vault, or a backup. Not one moment of Lumi's life is
written into that cloth.

**The scarf assigns memories an address.**

The technique is ancient and entirely mundane in its origins: Keepers
discovered, thousands of years before anyone could say why, that a mind holds
onto experience far better when each experience is given a *place*. Not a
category. A place. A location you can walk to.

So a Keeper's memories are hung on the constellation.

The smell of cut grass from a summer before you could speak — that lives beside
the Meadow fastening, because that is where you put it, at six years old,
without deciding to.

A lullaby whose tune you know and whose words you do not — Moon.

The first snowfall you were ever frightened by, and the person who told you it
was only weather — Frost.

The face of someone who mattered — wherever you put it.

Over a lifetime the arrangement becomes a **map of a self**. Not the contents.
The **index**.

And here is the cruelty of what happened in the Star Vault.

When the constellation broke, Lumi's memories were not destroyed. Every one of
them is still there, intact, unharmed, sitting exactly where it was left.

**The addresses were destroyed.**

The library is whole. The catalogue is ash. The shelves are full and none of
them are labelled and there is no longer any such thing as a direction.

---

## WHAT AMNESIA TOOK AND WHAT IT LEFT

This distinction matters enormously, both for the story and for what Lumi can
still do, and it maps precisely onto how memory actually fails in living
creatures.

**LOST — the addressed kind.** Names. Faces. Places. *Sequence.* The entire
autobiographical record: who he is, who he knew, where he has been, what
happened to him and in what order. He cannot retrieve a single specific episode
of his own life. Not one.

**KEPT — the unaddressed kind.** Everything his body learned rather than his
history. How to fight. How to fall. How to knot a scarf in the dark without
looking. The tune of the lullaby — though not one word of it. How to hold still
when something small and frightened is deciding whether to trust you.

**And kept, most importantly: his character.**

Kindness is not an episode. It is not filed anywhere. It is not a thing you
remember — it is a thing you are, worn into you by ten thousand repetitions
until it became the shape of your hands.

Lumi wakes in a field with no idea who he is, and he is still, immediately,
recognisably, *himself*.

This is the quiet argument the whole story is making, and it is the argument
Noctra has spent four hundred years getting wrong:

> **You are not your memories. You are what your memories made you, and that
> survives them.**

---

## HOW THE MEMORIES COME BACK

Not by being told.

This is the rule that generates most of the story's pain, and it is not a
contrivance — it is how retrieval actually works.

If someone simply *tells* Lumi a fact about his past, he acquires a fact. He
now knows a sentence about a stranger who happens to share his name. It goes
into the unlabelled pile with everything else. Nothing lights up. Nothing comes
home.

A memory returns when its **address is restored** — when Lumi stands in the
right place, or hears the right sound, or when a Star settles back into its
fastening and a whole constellation-quadrant of his life abruptly has
coordinates again.

Which means Noctra — who knows everything, who is the only living creature who
can answer every question Lumi has — **cannot help him.**

He can talk for a hundred years and Lumi will not remember one thing.

The only way Lumi gets his life back is by walking to ten places and being
worth answering in each of them.

And the only thing Noctra can do about it is **follow him and wait**.

---

## THE LINE OF KEEPERS

There have been ten Starkeepers.

The ninth was **Aurelle** — a white cat, small, unremarkable to look at, the
finest Keeper the tradition ever produced and the one who ended it. She held the
office during the Long Bright. She performed the binding at the First Eclipse.
She wrote the history afterward.

Then she took off her scarf, hung it on nothing, and the line broke for four
hundred years.

The tenth is Lumi.

He was not appointed. He was not born to it. No council selected him, no
ceremony marked him, and no living creature taught him the Refusals.

The gold filigree on his forelegs and brow — the Keeper's mark — is not a
birthmark. It is **earned**; it appears, gradually, in those who do the work,
and Lumi had it before anyone in Veylora knew he existed.

Somebody trained him.

Underground.

In the dark.

Through a wall he could not see.
