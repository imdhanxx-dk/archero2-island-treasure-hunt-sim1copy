# IV. LUMI — THE CAT WITH THE EMPTY SKY

## What he is

A small white cat. Genuinely small — the kind of small that makes people finish
their sentence before they look down and realise who they were talking to.

Pale gold eyes. Soft, slightly untidy fur with a cream undertone at the ears and
tail-tip. Thin lines of gold filigree running along his forelegs and across his
brow, dormant most of the time, lighting from within when he works.

And a long dark-indigo scarf, frayed at both ends, with ten small silver
fastenings stitched along it in a pattern that exists nowhere in the sky.

He does not look like the tenth holder of one of the two oldest offices in the
world, and this is not false modesty or a disguise. He genuinely is not
impressive. The tradition has never once selected for impressive. It selects for
**the quality of being answered**, and nobody has ever found a way to tell by
looking.

---

## Where he came from

Nobody knows. Including him. Including, on his worse days, Noctra.

A kitten was found at the edge of Eclipse Sanctuary — the one place in Veylora
nothing lives, nothing goes, and nothing has any business being. No tracks
leading in. No one looking for him. Not abandoned so much as *arrived*.

The obvious theory is that he is descended from Aurelle's line. It is never
confirmed. The story is better if it stays unconfirmed, because the point of
Lumi is that he was not owed the office by blood — he got it by being the only
creature in four centuries who sat down at the edge of a sealed place and
listened to it.

---

## The voice underground

Here is the thing Lumi cannot remember, and the reason the story has teeth.

**Noctra raised him.**

From inside the seal, Noctra could reach the surface in exactly one way: as
sound, in darkness, when no one was listening. Not a body. Not a face. A voice
coming up through roots and stone and the space under the world, available only
after sunset, only to someone sitting still in a place everyone else avoided.

So a very small cat with nowhere else to be sat at the edge of Eclipse Sanctuary
every evening for years, and something down there talked to him.

It taught him the Four Refusals — all four, including the one that was struck
from the oath, which is a detail no scholar alive could explain.

It taught him how to fall without breaking. How to stand in a place where
something is frightened and not be the largest thing in it. How to knot a scarf
in the dark without looking.

It taught him **the Ledger** — how to find where the cost landed.

And it taught him, with an insistence that made no sense at the time, that you
do not have to destroy a thing to stop it.

It sang to him. The same song, most nights. A low, slow, unhurried thing in a
language Lumi never learned the words of.

**And when the voice judged he was ready, it sent up a scarf.**

Woven in the dark galleries. Eclipse-silk. Ten fastenings. The last thing the
Keeper of the Interval ever made, pushed up through four hundred feet of root
and rock as a gift to a stray who had no idea what he was being handed.

Then the voice said goodbye and went quiet, and Lumi walked out into Veylora
with a Nightkeeper's masterwork around his neck and a head full of an office
nobody had told him the name of.

The Stars found him within the year. All ten. Which had not happened in four
hundred years, and which every scholar in Mirrowen attributed to destiny,
prophecy, or luck.

It was none of those.

**The Stars came because they recognised the weaving.**

---

## Why he will not kill

This is the single most important fact about Lumi, and its origin is the cruel
joke the entire story is built on.

In the Star Vault, under a creature that could end him in one motion, Lumi
fights **entirely defensively**. Barriers. Deflections. Light shaped to *push
away* rather than burn. He never once tries to land a killing blow, and Noctra
— who has fought a great many people — notices within seconds, because it is
strange and it is deliberate and it is costing Lumi the fight.

*"Why won't you fight?"*

*"Because you're angry. That doesn't mean you're supposed to die."*

Lumi does not know where he learned that.

Noctra does.

**He taught it to him.** Through a wall. For years. Because in four hundred
years of dark, the one thing the Keeper of the Interval most wanted to exist in
the world was a creature who would refuse to do to someone else what was done to
him.

He built the thing that would stop him.

And then he forgot he had — not the fact, he remembers the fact perfectly. He
forgot what it would *feel like* to have it turned on him. He walked into that
Vault expecting a Keeper, and what he got was his own voice, coming out of
someone else's mouth, telling him he did not deserve to die.

That is why the gem cracked gold.

That is why he ran.

---

## The amnesia, precisely

When the constellation broke, the index burned and the library survived.

**GONE — everything with an address.**

- Every name. Including his own, for the first several hours.
- Every face. He does not know what Aurelle looked like; he never met her, but
  he also does not know that he never met her, which is its own kind of vertigo.
- Every place, as a *place he has been*. He can recognise a road. He cannot
  recognise that he has walked it.
- **Sequence.** This is the worst one and the least depicted. He has no before
  and after. Even when fragments surface, they arrive unsorted — a black
  silhouette, violet eyes, stars tearing through a ceiling — with no way to
  know whether that happened last week or in childhood or at all.

**KEPT — everything with no address at all.**

- How to fight. His body knows forms his mind has never heard of.
- How to fall, roll, land.
- How to knot the scarf in the dark. His paws do it while he is thinking about
  something else, and it unsettles him every time.
- **The tune of the lullaby.** Not one word. He hums it when he is frightened
  and does not notice he is doing it.
- The Four Refusals, as *instincts* rather than rules. He does not know he is
  obeying an oath. He simply finds that certain things are not available to him.
- His kindness. Which is not a memory, and therefore could not be taken.

He wakes in a field having lost his entire life, and within ninety seconds he is
apologising to a frightened plant-creature for existing too loudly near it.

**That is the argument.** Strip a person of every episode of their history and
what is left is not a blank. What is left is the shape their history wore into
them — and that is the part that was actually them the whole time.

---

## What the empty fastenings do

Nothing. That is the point, and it should be played as grief rather than
inconvenience.

Ten small silver rings on a strip of woven night. He touches them. Nothing
happens. He touches another. Nothing. He keeps doing it, absently, for the
entire first act, the way you keep checking a pocket for something you already
know is not there.

He has no abilities worth the name at the start. The gold filigree is dark. The
barriers do not come.

And each Star's return does two things at once, which is why the structure
works:

1. A verb comes back to him — he can *continue*, or *carry*, or *keep*.
2. **A quadrant of his life gets its addresses back.** Not all of it. The
   region of himself that was filed beside that fastening.

So the Meadow Star does not just return growth to him. It returns *the smell of
cut grass from before he could speak*, and the sudden, staggering knowledge that
he was once small and safe somewhere.

He does not get his memory back in a lump at the end. He gets it back in ten
pieces, each one changing what the previous ones meant, and the final piece —
Shadow — recontextualises all nine.

---

## The shape of him, for anyone writing him

**He is funny.** This is not decoration. A character who has lost everything and
is still making jokes at his own expense is making a *choice*, every time, and
the audience should be able to feel the choice.

He falls over. He talks to animals that do not answer. He narrates his own
humiliations to an empty field. *"Nobody saw that."* This is how he stays
upright.

**He apologises to things that cannot hear him.** Doors. Rocks. A patch of moss
he stepped on. It is a tic, it is very small, and it is the single clearest
tell that somebody once taught him that the unnoticed things count.

**He asks questions instead of making claims.** Partly amnesia. Mostly training.

**He is not naive, and the story must not let anyone call him naive without
being wrong.** His refusal to destroy Noctra is not innocence. It is the hardest
discipline in the book, executed under pressure, by someone who cannot even
remember why he believes it. That is *more* impressive than if he remembered.

**And he is lonely in a way he cannot locate.** He does not miss anyone, because
missing requires knowing who. He simply carries a constant low sense that there
is a conversation he is supposed to be having, and he keeps turning around to
see who is behind him.

Somebody usually is.
